I am a Research Engineer II at Microsoft, working on LLM evaluation, model post-training, and enterprise security retrieval. My research interests center on understanding model failures and building reliable, efficient language systems.

My earlier research spans multimodal learning, speech, and machine translation, with work at the University of Toronto’s Matter Lab, Samsung R&D, and the University of Hamburg. I hold a dual degree in Computer Science and Chemistry from BITS Pilani.

Research

Evaluation and reliability.

I evaluate smaller open-weight LLM judges against larger-model baselines across multilingual criteria. My work includes rubric design, systematic failure analysis, and experiments on guardrail behavior and regressions.

Retrieval and robustness.

I build adversarial retrieval benchmarks using synthetic data, semantic rewrites, and character obfuscation. I also collaborated with Microsoft Research to evaluate GraphRAG and LazyGraphRAG for enterprise security retrieval.

Post-training and efficiency.

I develop post-training pipelines using supervised fine-tuning, distillation, and quantization. I also investigate context management for tool-using voice agents and evaluate inference strategies for latency and throughput.
